📄 storm-research skill anatomy

How the skill works under the hood: two files, frontmatter that triggers it, and safeguards that make the briefing reliable.

0 of 6
What it is:

The skill consists of two files in a folder: SKILL.md (the pipeline in text) and report-template.html (the report template).

Why learn:

Understanding that "skill = two files" demystifies installation and shows exactly what to copy.

Key concepts:

SKILL.md · report-template.html · skill folder.

What it is:

The block at the top of the SKILL.md with name, description e argument-hint; a description is the trigger Claude reads to invoke the skill.

Why learn:

É a description that triggers STORM when you ask for research—understanding this helps avoid "why didn't it run?".

Key concepts:

name · description · argument-hint · trigger.

What it is:

The skill uses only native tools (Agent, Write, web search/fetch); no external scripts, APIs, or paid services.

Why learn:

Because it’s self-contained, just drop the folder into .claude/skills/ and it works on any machine.

Key concepts:

Self-contained · portable · native tools.

What it is:

report-template.html is the report’s visual template; the pipeline clones and fills it in without rewriting the CSS.

Why learn:

Keeping the template intact ensures clean, consistent reports—Montserrat, Roboto Mono, and blue highlight.

Key concepts:

Template · clone · visual identity.

What it is:

A run creates about 9 to 11 agents: 5 perspectives in Phase 1 and ~4 to 6 verifiers in Phase 4.

Why learn:

Knowing the expected cost avoids surprises and keeps you from inflating the panel beyond what’s recommended.

Key concepts:

Cost · 5 perspectives · 1 verifier per cluster.

What it is:

The skill's safety rules: real sources only, nothing made up, and anything that can't be verified is downgraded or cut.

Why learn:

These safeguards are what make the briefing reliable — they’re the method’s "promise kept."

Key concepts:

Real research · downgrade · verification required.

⬇️ Install the skill

Download the files, drop them into the right folder, and confirm that Claude recognizes the skill — including common errors that trip up beginners.

0 of 6
What it is:

The four files to download: SKILL.md e report-template.html, in Portuguese and English.

Why learn:

This is the starting point — without the files, there’s no skill to install. This module includes the download links.

Key concepts:

Download · PT/EN · skill + template.

What it is:

Both files go together in .claude/skills/storm-research/, in your project or on your home page.

Why learn:

The right folder is what lets Claude Code find and recognize the skill.

Key concepts:

.claude/skills · skill folder · files together.

What it is:

The same skill works with other agents (Codex and similar tools); only the folder changes— .codex/ or .agents/.

Why learn:

Shows that the skill is portable and that you aren’t tied to a single agent.

Key concepts:

Portable · .codex · .agents.

What it is:

A simple prompt for Claude to list the skills and confirm that storm-research appears.

Why learn:

Confirm the installation worked before you rely on it.

Key concepts:

List skills · recognition · check.

What it is:

Tell Claude who you are and what your business is, so the reports are tailored to your role.

Why learn:

Context makes the actionable insight speak directly to your reality, not to a generic person.

Key concepts:

Context · target reader · tailored.

What it is:

Typical failures: wrong folder, only the SKILL.md without the template, or file permissions.

Why learn:

Knowing the common errors resolves most “it didn’t work” issues in seconds.

Key concepts:

Wrong folder · missing template · permissions.

⚙️ The 4-phase pipeline

From scope to delivery: the phases that turn a topic into a verified briefing — scope, five lenses, contradiction map, synthesis, and verification.

0 of 6
What it is:

Claude reads the topic, summarizes it in one line, identifies the target reader, and derives a topic-slug.

Why learn:

A good scope targets the practical section to the right reader and names the final file.

Key concepts:

Interpretation · target reader · topic slug.

What it is:

Five general-purpose agents launched in a single message, each with the same frame and its own lens, in fewer than 400 words.

Why learn:

This is the multi-perspective heart of the method — and running in parallel is what makes the panel practical.

Key concepts:

Parallel · general-purpose · shared frame.

What it is:

Claude cross-checks the five briefs and identifies direct conflicts, strong versus weak evidence, universal agreement, and blind spots.

Why learn:

This is the raw material for the findings and the report’s “supports/challenges” chips.

Key concepts:

Direct conflict · universal agreement · blind spot.

What it is:

Clone report-template.html and fill in the summary, 5 findings, hidden connection, 6th lens, insight, boundary, and references.

Why learn:

This is where the research becomes a readable report, saved in storm-reports/.

Key concepts:

Clone template · sections · storm-reports.

What it is:

Self-evaluation (scores from 1–10, weakest link, bias, 6th lens) plus ~4 to 6 parallel verifiers checking each citation.

Why learn:

This is what separates STORM from an ordinary report; without it, the briefing isn’t reliable.

Key concepts:

Adversarial · verification · banner.

What it is:

The post-verification deliverable: the HTML v2, opened with xdg-open, plus a summary in the chat (scorecard, universal finding, boundary).

Why learn:

Knowing what to expect in the output helps you judge the quality of what you received.

Key concepts:

v2 · scorecard · universal finding.

🧭 Your first report

In practice: choose a good topic, launch the pipeline, follow the lenses, open the HTML, and iterate until you have a better version.

0 of 6
What it is:

A good topic has multiple points of view and checkable claims—not a simple factual query.

Why learn:

The right topic accounts for half the result; a shallow topic wastes the entire panel.

Key concepts:

Contested · checkable · suitability.

What it is:

A simple command—"Run a Storm Research on <seu tópico>. The reader is <seu papel>." — with the variable parts marked.

Why learn:

This is the actual trigger; knowing how to fill it in is what gets the pipeline running.

Key concepts:

Prompt · variables · target reader.

What it is:

During Phase 1, you see 5 subagents running in parallel; you can click to view each one's prompt.

Why learn:

Following shows that the research is real and helps you trust the result.

Key concepts:

Subagents · parallel · transparency.

What it is:

At the end of Phase 1, Claude posts 2 to 3 lines saying where the lenses converge and what the strongest divergence is.

Why learn:

This is an honest summary of the panel — not a dump of raw briefings into the chat.

Key concepts:

Convergence · divergence · synthesis.

What it is:

The report is in storm-reports/<slug>-briefing.html; open with xdg-open (Linux), open (macOS) or start "" (Windows).

Why learn:

Knowing how to open the file is the last step in reading your briefing.

Key concepts:

xdg-open · path · HTML briefing.

What it is:

You can request a new version—for example: "run the missing 6th lens and generate a V3".

Why learn:

Iterating refines the report and explores angles the first pass missed.

Key concepts:

Iteration · 6th lens · V2/V3.
